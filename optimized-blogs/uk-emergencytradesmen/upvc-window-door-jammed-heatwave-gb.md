    <p class="blog-intro">There is a very specific kind of British frustration that only happens during a summer heatwave. You finally get the kids to sleep, the house feels like a greenhouse, and you go to open the bedroom window to let in a breeze. You push the handle, and it doesn’t budge. You try the patio door to step outside, but the handle won’t lift high enough to lock it. You push, you pull, you jiggle the handle, and absolutely nothing happens. At this point, you’re not just sweating from the 30-degree heat; you’re trapped in your own home by a piece of white plastic. I’ve stood at that exact window, pressing my face against the glass, wondering if I should just sleep in the bath. But before you start violently yanking the handle or ringing an emergency locksmith in a panic, take a steady breath. uPVC windows and doors jamming in the heat is incredibly common, and it’s almost never a broken lock. Plastic expands in direct sunlight. When the frame swells, the locking rollers no longer line up with the metal keeps on the frame. With a few safe, gentle checks, you can usually free the mechanism without snapping the gearbox. Let’s walk through it step by step.</p>

    <h2>1. UK uPVC Jammed Lock: The "Knowledge Capsule" Summary</h2>
    <div class="capsule-box">
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Heat Expansion:</strong> uPVC plastic expands roughly 10 times more than aluminium, causing misalignments up to 3mm in direct sunlight.</li>
            <li><strong>Stop Forcing:</strong> Forcing a stuck multipoint lock handle can snap or shear the internal gearbox, resulting in a £200 replacement.</li>
            <li><strong>Cool the Frame:</strong> Wiping the frame with a damp, cool cloth shrinks the plastic enough to let the lock align and release naturally.</li>
            <li><strong>Lubrication Rules:</strong> Spray metal locking strips with silicone or dry PTFE spray twice a year; never use WD-40, which attracts dirt.</li>
            <li><strong>Keep Adjustments:</strong> Modern uPVC keeps have adjustable screws to accommodate summer thermal expansion without destructive entry.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
    <p>In the United Kingdom, exterior uPVC doors and windows are extremely susceptible to thermal movement during summer hot spells because they are exposed to direct sunlight for hours. When the frame absorbs heat, the PVC profile swells, causing the locking hooks and rollers to grind against their keeps rather than gliding in smoothly. Let's look at how to cool the plastic and safely release the mechanism.</p>

    <h2>2. Step 1: Stop Forcing the Handle Immediately</h2>
    <div class="blog-step-card">
        <div class="blog-step-header">
            <span class="blog-step-number">Step 1</span>
            <h2>Cease Upward or Downward Pressure to Protect Gearbox</h2>
        </div>
        <div class="blog-step-body">
            <p>The moment a uPVC multipoint lock sticks, the instinct is to force the handle up or down. Don’t. The internal gearbox is made of relatively soft metal. If the locking bolts are misaligned due to the frame expanding, forcing the handle will strip the teeth inside the gearbox or snap the spindle.</p>
            <p>If the handle feels stiff, release it. Repeated stress on a heat-swollen frame is the fastest way to turn a temporary thermal issue into a £200 lock replacement.</p>
        </div>
    </div>

    <h2>3. Step 2: Cool the Frame Down Gently</h2>
    <div class="blog-step-card">
        <div class="blog-step-header">
            <span class="blog-step-number">Step 2</span>
            <h2>Apply a Damp, Cool Cloth to the Swollen uPVC Sash</h2>
        </div>
        <div class="blog-step-body">
            <p>If the window or door is in direct, blazing sunlight, the uPVC profile can expand by up to 3mm. That tiny shift is enough to throw the lock out of alignment. To fix this, you need to cool the plastic.</p>
            <p>Take a clean cloth, run it under cold tap water, wring it out so it’s damp (not dripping), and wipe it over the frame and the edge of the door or window sash. Do not use ice cubes or freezing water, as extreme temperature shock can crack the double-glazed glass unit. Once the frame cools down slightly, try operating the handle gently.</p>
        </div>
    </div>

    <blockquote class="expert-tip">
        <p><strong>Expert Tip:</strong> Wipe down the rubber gaskets with a damp cloth to remove grit that causes friction when closing. Dirty gaskets increase drag and resistance on the multipoint lock.</p>
    </blockquote>

    <h2>4. Step 3: Check the Hinges and Alignment</h2>
    <div class="blog-step-card">
        <div class="blog-step-header">
            <span class="blog-step-number">Step 3</span>
            <h2>Inspect the Sash Gap and Lift the Sash</h2>
        </div>
        <div class="blog-step-body">
            <p>If cooling the frame doesn’t work, the heavy sash may have dropped slightly on its hinges, dragging the bottom edge on the frame. Open the window or door just a fraction of an inch if possible (or look closely if it’s partially open). Is the gap at the top wider than the gap at the bottom? If the sash has dropped, the locking hooks won’t engage. You can often lift the sash slightly with your fingers while turning the handle to relieve the pressure on the locking mechanism.</p>
        </div>
    </div>

    <div class="fun-fact">
        <p><strong>Fun Fact!</strong> uPVC (unplasticized polyvinyl chloride) expands roughly 10 times more than aluminium and significantly more than timber when exposed to the same heat.</p>
    </div>

    <h2>5. Step 4: Lubricate the Moving Parts</h2>
    <div class="blog-step-card">
        <div class="blog-step-header">
            <span class="blog-step-number">Step 4</span>
            <h2>Apply Silicone-Based Lubricant or PTFE Spray</h2>
        </div>
        <div class="blog-step-body">
            <p>Heat dries out factory grease, and dust sticks to it, creating a gritty paste that jams the lock. If you can get the window open even slightly, spray a silicone-based lubricant or dry PTFE spray along the metal locking strip on the edge of the sash, and into the metal keeps on the frame. Work the handle up and down gently to distribute the spray.</p>
            <p>Never use WD-40 or oil-based sprays on uPVC multipoint locks. They attract dirt, degrade the rubber seals, and will make the lock stiffer in the long run.</p>
        </div>
    </div>

    <h2>6. Step 5: Adjust the Locking Keeps (If Comfortable)</h2>
    <div class="blog-step-card">
        <div class="blog-step-header">
            <span class="blog-step-number">Step 5</span>
            <h2>Perform Minor Allen Key Adjustments to Keeps</h2>
        </div>
        <div class="blog-step-body">
            <p>If the door or window consistently jams every time the sun hits it, the metal keeps (the slots on the frame where the lock hooks engage) might need a tiny adjustment. Most modern keeps have a small Allen key screw inside them. You can loosen this screw and move the keep up, down, or in/out by a millimetre to accommodate the summer expansion.</p>
            <p>If you aren’t confident doing this, wait until the evening when the temperature drops. The plastic will naturally contract, and the lock will likely free itself.</p>
        </div>
    </div>

    <h2>7. Practical Advice to Prevent Summer uPVC Jams</h2>
    <div class="blog-tips-block">
        <h2>Four Essential Summer uPVC Care Rules</h2>
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Bi-Annual Cleaning:</strong> Clean the locking strip and hinges twice a year with a silicone spray to keep the mechanism moving smoothly.</li>
            <li><strong>Wipe Rubber Gaskets:</strong> Wipe down the rubber gaskets with a damp cloth to remove grit that causes friction when closing.</li>
            <li><strong>Avoid Door Slamming:</strong> Never slam uPVC doors or windows. The shock can misalign the hinges and drop the sash.</li>
            <li><strong>Reduce Heat Exposure:</strong> Keep blinds or curtains closed during the hottest part of the day to reduce direct thermal expansion on the frames.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>

    <h2>8. When to Call an Emergency Tradesman (And When to Repair vs. Replace)</h2>
    <div class="blog-when-block">
        <h2>Five Locksmith & Joinery Crises That Need Immediate Expert Attention</h2>
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Sheared Gearbox:</strong> The handle spins 360 degrees freely without engaging the lock (broken gearbox).</li>
            <li><strong>Snapped Key:</strong> The key snaps inside the euro cylinder barrel.</li>
            <li><strong>Severe Dropping:</strong> The door has dropped severely and is scraping the threshold, preventing you from securing the house.</li>
            <li><strong>Securing Before Nightfall:</strong> You need a same-day repair to secure your home before nightfall.</li>
            <li><strong>Warped Frame Profile:</strong> The uPVC sash itself has physically bowed due to lack of steel core, requiring professional heating or replacement.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>

    </div>
    <p>Repair vs. Replace: The Quick Rule. If the internal gearbox has snapped because someone forced the handle in the heat, a locksmith can usually replace just the gearbox or the entire multipoint locking strip for £100–£180. You do not need a whole new door or window. However, if the uPVC frame itself has permanently warped or bowed due to lack of internal steel reinforcement (common in very cheap, old installations), the entire sash may need replacing.</p>

    <h2>9. 🔍 Quick Q&A: Real Questions Homeowners Ask</h2>
    <div class="blog-qa-section">
        <h2>uPVC Summer Jam Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
        <div class="blog-qa-card">
            <p class="blog-qa-q">Q: Why do my uPVC doors and windows jam in the summer?</p>
            <ul>
                <li>uPVC plastic expands in direct heat and sunlight.</li>
                <li>The expansion misaligns the locking hooks with the metal keeps.</li>
                <li>Dried-out grease and dust create friction in the mechanism.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>
        <div class="blog-qa-card">
            <p class="blog-qa-q">Q: Can I pour cold water on the frame to shrink it?</p>
            <ul>
                <li>Use a damp, cool cloth, not pouring water or ice.</li>
                <li>Sudden extreme temperature changes can cause the double-glazed glass to crack (thermal shock).</li>
                <li>Gentle cooling is enough to shrink the plastic by the required millimetres.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>
        <div class="blog-qa-card">
            <p class="blog-qa-q">Q: Why does the handle spin but the door won’t open?</p>
            <ul>
                <li>The internal multipoint gearbox has sheared or snapped.</li>
                <li>The spindle connecting the handle to the lock has broken.</li>
                <li>This requires a locksmith to strip the lock and fit a new gearbox.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>
        <div class="blog-qa-card">
            <p class="blog-qa-q">Q: How much does it cost to fix a jammed uPVC door lock?</p>
            <ul>
                <li>A simple realignment and lubrication callout is usually £60–£90.</li>
                <li>Replacing a failed multipoint locking strip or gearbox typically costs £120–£200.</li>
                <li>Replacing a euro cylinder (where the key goes) is around £80–£120.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>
    </div>

    <div class="blog-fun-facts">
        <h2>Two Fast Fun Facts About uPVC Expansion</h2>
        <ul>
            <li>uPVC (unplasticized polyvinyl chloride) expands roughly 10 times more than aluminium and significantly more than timber when exposed to the same heat.</li>
            <li>Modern uPVC windows are reinforced with internal steel or aluminium cores to prevent warping, but the outer plastic layer still expands, which is why alignment is so critical.</li>
        </ul>
