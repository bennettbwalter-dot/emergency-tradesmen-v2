    <h2>3. LiFePO4 vs. NMC: The 2026 Standard for Battery Longevity</h2>
    <div class="capsule-box">
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Cycle Life:</strong> LiFePO4 units last 7x longer (10+ years of daily use) than the Lithium-ion batteries found in 2020-era power stations.</li>
            <li><strong>Thermal Stability:</strong> LiFePO4 chemistry is significantly more resistant to "thermal runaway," making it the safest choice for indoor storage.</li>
            <li><strong>Cost over Time:</strong> While slightly heavier and more expensive upfront, the "cost per cycle" for LiFePO4 in 2026 is actually 60% lower than older tech.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
    <p>Why does battery chemistry matter for a UK homeowner? Because in 2026, many households are using these units for "Peak Topping"-charging them during cheap overnight "Agile" rates and using the power during expensive peak hours (4pm-7pm). This daily cycling would kill an older NMC battery in less than two years. A 2026-standard LiFePO4 unit can handle this abuse for over a decade, effectively paying for itself through energy bill savings while providing emergency backup as a "free" secondary benefit.</p>

A high-capacity portable power station with an AC outlet can run a modern UK combi-boiler for several hours, provided it is safely connected via a professional transfer switch.     <div class="capsule-box">

A portable power station with UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply) capability acts as a filter. It takes the "dirty" grid power, converts it to DC, and then "re-makes" pure sine wave AC for your devices. This dual-conversion approach ensures that your expensive UK home office gear is protected from the grid as much as it is powered by the battery.</p>

    <div class="dos-donts">
        <h3>Portable Power Safety: Things You CAN and CANNOT Do</h3>
        <ul>
            <li><strong>CAN:</strong> Use your station to power medical devices like CPAP machines or nebulizers. Ensure you have at least 2kWh of capacity for 10+ hours of run-time.</li>
            <li><strong>CANNOT:</strong> Use a "Suicide Lead" (male-to-male) to plug a power station into a wall socket. This is illegal under UK BS 7671 and can kill a utility worker.</li>
            <li><strong>CANNOT:</strong> Store your power station in an unheated UK garage or loft during winter. Cold temperatures (below 0°C) can damage the chemical structure of the cells.</li>
            <li><strong>CANNOT:</strong> Overload the "Inverter." If you try to run a 3,000W kettle on a 500W unit, the internal safety breaker will trip to prevent a fire.</li>
        </ul>

    <h2>8. BS 7671:2026 Amendment 4: New Safety and Isolation Rules</h2>
    <div class="capsule-box">
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Labeling:</strong> Any home with integrated battery backup must have clear "Stored Energy" labels on the main consumer unit.</li>
            <li><strong>Isolation:</strong> Both the AC and DC sides of a battery system must have dedicated, reachable isolation switches for emergency services.</li>
            <li><strong>Verification:</strong> If your unit is "hard-wired" via a transfer switch, it must be inspected by a <strong>NIC EIC</strong> or <strong>NAPIT</strong> verified electrician.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
    <p>Amendment 4 of the 18th Edition (BS 7671) was released in 2026 specifically to address the surge in home battery storage. It clarifies that a portable power station is a "Generating Set" if it's connected to the premises' wiring. This means you cannot just "DIY" a connection to your fuse board. For the safety of your family and the UK grid, any permanent integration must meet the same strict standards as a fixed solar battery or a standby generator.</p>

    <h2>9. Stationary Secondary Batteries: Understanding Chapter 702</h2>
    <div class="capsule-box">
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Location:</strong> Batteries must be kept in areas with adequate ventilation to prevent heat build-up.</li>
            <li><strong>Fire Safety:</strong> Systems exceeding 5kWh capacity now require dedicated fire-resistant segregation in UK domestic properties.</li>
            <li><strong>Earthing:</strong> When running in "Island Mode" (grid down), your system must have its own path to earth, typically through a dedicated earth rod.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
    <p>Chapter 702 is the new section of the UK wiring regs dedicated to "Secondary Batteries." It focuses on the risks of stored energy. Unlike a kettle, which is only dangerous when on, a battery is "always-on" potential energy. In 2026, we ensure that every portable station we use for home integration is installed with a "Switched Neutral" transfer switch. This ensures that your home's earth-neutral bond remains safe even when the grid is completely disconnected.</p>
