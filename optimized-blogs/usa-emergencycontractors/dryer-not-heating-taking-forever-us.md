<p class="blog-intro">There is a very specific kind of laundry-day betrayal that happens when you pull a load out of the dryer expecting warm, fluffy towels and instead get a damp, heavy sack that smells like wet dog. You run it again. Thirty minutes later, same result. The drum is spinning, the light is on, but the heat is completely missing. At this point, your dryer isn’t an appliance; it’s a very expensive, very loud clothes hamper. I’ve stood in that exact utility room, staring at a pile of damp jeans, wondering if I should start hanging things on shower rods like I’m back in college. But before you start pulling the back panel off or frantically searching “24/7 appliance repair near me,” take a slow breath. Dryers that won’t heat or take forever to dry are almost always pointing to one of three things: restricted airflow, a tripped thermal fuse, or a simple power issue. With a few safe, logical checks, you can often restore heat and avoid a serious fire hazard. Let’s walk through it step by step.</p>

    <div class="blog-step-card">
      <div class="blog-step-header">
        <span class="blog-step-number">Step 1</span>
        <h2>Clean the Lint Screen & Check the Exhaust Vent</h2>
      </div>
      <div class="blog-step-body">
        <p>Restricted airflow is the #1 reason dryers stop heating or run endlessly. Lint builds up fast, and when hot air can’t escape, the dryer overheats and shuts off the heating element as a safety measure. Clean the lint screen thoroughly before every load. Next, pull the dryer away from the wall and disconnect the exhaust duct. Check for heavy lint buildup, crushed flexible hose, or bird nests in the exterior vent cap. Use a vent brush or vacuum to clear the line. Never run a dryer with a blocked vent. Lint is highly flammable, and clogged ducts cause thousands of house fires every year.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="blog-step-card">
      <div class="blog-step-header">
        <span class="blog-step-number">Step 2</span>
        <h2>Verify the Power Supply (Electric vs Gas)</h2>
      </div>
      <div class="blog-step-body">
        <p>If your dryer is electric, it runs on a dedicated 240-volt circuit. If one leg of power drops, the drum will spin but the heater won’t engage. Check your breaker panel for a double-pole breaker labeled “Dryer.” If one side is tripped, flip both fully off, then back on. Test the outlet with a multimeter if you’re comfortable, or plug in a known-working 240V device. If you have a gas dryer, ensure the gas valve behind the unit is fully open. Check that the pilot or electronic igniter is firing. Never force a gas valve or ignore the smell of gas. If you smell rotten eggs, leave the house and call your gas company immediately.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="blog-step-card">
      <div class="blog-step-header">
        <span class="blog-step-number">Step 3</span>
        <h2>Inspect the Thermal Fuse & Heating Element</h2>
      </div>
      <div class="blog-step-body">
        <p>Dryers have a thermal fuse that blows if the unit overheats. Once it blows, the dryer will run but produce zero heat. Unplug the dryer completely. Remove the back panel (usually held by a few screws) and locate the thermal fuse on the blower housing or exhaust path. Visually check for burn marks or a broken filament. If you have a multimeter, test for continuity. No continuity means the fuse is blown and must be replaced. While you’re back there, check the heating element coils for visible breaks or scorching. Do not bypass the thermal fuse. It’s a critical fire safety device.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="blog-step-card">
      <div class="blog-step-header">
        <span class="blog-step-number">Step 4</span>
        <h2>Clean the Moisture Sensors & Drum Seals</h2>
      </div>
      <div class="blog-step-body">
        <p>Modern dryers use metal moisture sensor bars inside the drum to detect when clothes are dry. If they’re coated in dryer sheet residue or fabric softener buildup, they misread dampness and shut off heat prematurely. Unplug the unit, locate the sensor bars (usually near the lint filter housing), and wipe them firmly with rubbing alcohol and a clean cloth. Check the front and rear drum seals for tears or gaps. Worn seals let hot air escape into the cabinet instead of circulating through the clothes, which drastically increases dry times and strains the motor.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="blog-step-card">
      <div class="blog-step-header">
        <span class="blog-step-number">Step 5</span>
        <h2>Run a Test Cycle & Monitor Airflow</h2>
      </div>
      <div class="blog-step-body">
        <p>Once the vent is clear, power is verified, and sensors are clean, plug the dryer back in and run a timed high-heat cycle with a small load. Stand outside and check the exterior vent cap. You should feel strong, steady hot air pushing out. If airflow is weak, the duct run is too long, has too many bends, or is still partially blocked. If the dryer heats for 10 minutes then goes cold again, the cycling thermostat or high-limit switch is likely failing. At that point, stop troubleshooting and call a licensed appliance technician.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="blog-tips-block">
      <h2>Practical Advice to Prevent Dryer Failures</h2>
      <ul>
        <li>Clean the lint screen before every single load. It takes ten seconds and prevents most overheating issues.</li>
        <li>Replace plastic or foil accordion ducts with rigid or semi-rigid metal ducting. Flexible plastic traps lint and is a fire code violation in most states.</li>
        <li>Wipe moisture sensors monthly with rubbing alcohol to remove fabric softener film.</li>
        <li>Keep the dryer area well-ventilated. Overheating happens faster in hot, cramped utility closets.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>

    <div class="blog-when-block">
      <h2>When to Call a 24/7 Appliance Technician</h2>
      <p>You’ve done the safe checks, but some faults require a licensed professional immediately. Book a service call if:</p>
      <ul>
        <li>The dryer smells like burning plastic or hot metal</li>
        <li>The breaker trips repeatedly when you start a cycle</li>
        <li>The thermal fuse blows again shortly after replacement</li>
        <li>You have a gas dryer and the igniter won’t fire or you smell gas</li>
        <li>You need same-day repair to restore laundry function safely</li>
      </ul>
      <p>Never attempt to repair gas valves, 240V wiring, or internal control boards unless you are licensed and trained. Dryers combine high heat, electricity, and combustible lint. Incorrect repairs can cause fires or electrical shock. Let a certified technician handle it safely.</p>
    </div>

          <li>Failed heating element or gas igniter</li>

          <li>Coated moisture sensors shutting off heat early</li>
          <li>Overloaded drum preventing proper tumbling</li>
