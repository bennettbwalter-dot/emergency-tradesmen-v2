Spring storms in the US don’t knock politely. They kick the door in, drop a tree limb through your soffit, and leave you staring at a hole in your roof like you’re on the set of a low-budget disaster movie. One minute you’re checking the radar app, the next you’re holding a bucket under a skylight that wasn’t there yesterday, wondering if your homeowner’s insurance covers “acts of aggressive oak trees.” I’ve coordinated enough emergency board-ups to know that panic is the real enemy here. Before you start nailing plywood like a survivalist or Googling “24/7 emergency contractor near me” with wet hands, take a steady breath. Securing your home after storm damage is about smart, safe moves-not heroics. Let’s walk through exactly what to do right now so you can keep the weather out, protect your belongings, and get professional help fast.

<div class="blog-step-card">
  <div class="blog-step-header">
    <span class="blog-step-number">Step 1</span>
    <h2>Prioritize Safety Over Salvage</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="blog-step-body">
    <p>If a tree has hit your house, a window is shattered, or the roof is compromised, do not go inside until you’re sure it’s structurally sound. Watch for hanging power lines, gas smells, or sagging ceilings. If you smell gas, hear hissing, or see sparking wires, evacuate immediately and call 911 or your utility company. Turn off the main breaker and gas valve only if you can reach them safely from dry ground. Your home can be repaired. You cannot.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="blog-step-card">
  <div class="blog-step-header">
    <span class="blog-step-number">Step 2</span>
    <h2>Cover Openings Temporarily</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="blog-step-body">
    <p>Once the area is safe, stop water and debris from getting in. For broken windows or door glass, use thick plywood or OSB board cut slightly larger than the opening. Secure it from the outside using 2x4 braces and exterior screws. If you don’t have wood, heavy-duty moving blankets or thick cardboard taped securely can work for a few hours. For roof damage, use a waterproof tarp. Drape it over the damaged section, extend it over the ridge line, and secure it with wooden battens nailed into solid decking. Never nail directly into shingles or compromised wood.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="blog-step-card">
  <div class="blog-step-header">
    <span class="blog-step-number">Step 3</span>
    <h2>Document Everything for Insurance</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="blog-step-body">
    <p>Before you move debris or start cleaning, take clear photos and videos. Capture the exterior damage, interior water intrusion, fallen trees, and any ruined belongings. Note the date, time, and storm conditions. Most US homeowner policies cover sudden storm damage, but they require proof. Keep receipts for any temporary materials you buy (tarps, plywood, screws). These are often reimbursable as “mitigation expenses.”</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="blog-step-card">
  <div class="blog-step-header">
    <span class="blog-step-number">Step 4</span>
    <h2>Prevent Secondary Damage</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="blog-step-body">
    <p>Water is the real enemy after a storm. Move furniture, electronics, and rugs away from leaks. Place buckets under drips and poke a small hole in sagging ceiling drywall to release trapped water (this prevents a sudden collapse). Run fans and dehumidifiers if power is safe. Open windows on dry days to cross-ventilate. Mold can start growing within 48 hours, so drying out the space quickly is critical.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="blog-step-card">
  <div class="blog-step-header">
    <span class="blog-step-number">Step 5</span>
    <h2>Call a Licensed Emergency Contractor</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="blog-step-body">
    <p>Temporary covers are exactly that: temporary. Wind, rain, and temperature shifts will loosen tarps and plywood fast. Book a 24/7 emergency contractor who specializes in storm damage and board-up services. They have the equipment, insurance, and crew to secure your home properly, assess structural integrity, and coordinate with your insurance adjuster. Do not hire unlicensed “storm chasers” who show up uninvited. Verify credentials, ask for proof of insurance, and get everything in writing.</p>
  </div>

<div class="blog-tips-block">
  <h2>Practical Tips to Prepare for Future Storms</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>Keep an emergency kit ready: heavy-duty tarps, plywood sheets, exterior screws, work gloves, and a cordless drill.</li>
    <li>Trim trees near your roofline every 1–2 years. Overhanging limbs are the #1 cause of storm-related roof damage.</li>
    <li>Install impact-resistant windows or storm shutters if you live in tornado or hurricane-prone states.</li>
    <li>Review your homeowner’s policy annually. Ensure you have “replacement cost” coverage and understand your deductible.</li>
  </ul>
</div>
