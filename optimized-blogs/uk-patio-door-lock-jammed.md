    <p class="blog-intro">There is a very specific kind of British optimism that happens on the first warm day of spring. You make a cup of tea, walk over to the patio doors, and decide it’s time to let the garden in. You lift the handle. Nothing happens. You lift it harder. The mechanism groans, sticks halfway, and refuses to budge. You jiggle it, wiggle it, and briefly consider negotiating with the uPVC like it’s a stubborn toddler. Meanwhile, the sunshine is mocking you through the glass, and you’re trapped inside your own house like a contestant on a low-budget escape room.</p>

    <p class="blog-intro">I’ve stood at that exact door, tea going cold, wondering if I should start taking the hinges off with a butter knife. But before you start forcing the handle or ringing an emergency locksmith in a panic, take a steady breath. Jammed patio doors are almost never mysterious. They’re usually caused by a dropped door, dried-out mechanism, debris in the track, or thermal expansion from sudden spring heat. With a calm head and a few safe checks, you can often free the lock without damaging the frame. Let’s walk through it step by step, so you can get the doors open, secure your home, and avoid an unnecessary callout.</p>

    <div class="blog-step-card">
      <div class="blog-step-header">
        <span class="blog-step-number">Step 1</span>
        <h2>Stop Forcing the Handle</h2>
      </div>
      <div class="blog-step-body">
        <p>The moment a multipoint lock sticks, the instinct is to yank the handle harder. Don’t. uPVC and aluminium doors use a series of hooks, rollers, and bolts that run along the edge. Forcing a jammed handle can snap the gearbox, strip the spindle, or bend the locking strip. If the handle feels stiff or only moves halfway, release it gently. Repeated stress on a stuck mechanism is the fastest way to turn a simple adjustment into a full lock replacement.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="blog-step-card">
      <div class="blog-step-header">
        <span class="blog-step-number">Step 2</span>
        <h2>Check the Track & Rollers for Debris</h2>
      </div>
      <div class="blog-step-body">
        <p>Spring wind blows leaves, grit, and small stones straight into patio door tracks. Even a tiny pebble lodged under a roller can lift the door slightly, misaligning the lock bolts with the keep plates. Open the track cover if possible, or use a torch to inspect the bottom rail. Vacuum out dirt, then wipe the track with a damp cloth. Check the rollers at the bottom of the door. If they’re caked in grime or visibly worn, they won’t glide smoothly, which puts strain on the lock every time you lift the handle.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="blog-step-card">
      <div class="blog-step-header">
        <span class="blog-step-number">Step 3</span>
        <h2>Lubricate the Mechanism Safely</h2>
      </div>
      <div class="blog-step-body">
        <p>Dry locks stick. Over time, factory grease hardens, dust builds up, and the internal gears start to drag. Use a silicone-based spray or dry PTFE lubricant on the lock bolts, rollers, and hinge pins. Spray lightly, then work the handle up and down gently to distribute it. Never use WD-40 or oil-based sprays on uPVC multipoint locks. They attract dirt, gum up the gearbox, and can degrade plastic components. A quick silicone treatment often frees a stiff mechanism in under a minute.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="blog-step-card">
      <div class="blog-step-header">
        <span class="blog-step-number">Step 4</span>
        <h2>Check for Door Drop & Alignment</h2>
      </div>
      <div class="blog-step-body">
        <p>uPVC doors are heavy, and over time they can drop slightly on their hinges. When this happens, the lock bolts no longer line up with the metal keeps on the frame, causing the handle to jam when you try to lift it. Close the door and look at the gap around the edges. It should be even all the way round. If the door is dragging on the frame or sitting lower on the lock side, the hinges need adjusting. Most modern patio doors have Allen key adjusters on the hinges. A quarter-turn lift can realign the lock perfectly.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="blog-step-card">
      <div class="blog-step-header">
        <span class="blog-step-number">Step 5</span>
        <h2>Test the Multipoint System Gently</h2>
      </div>
      <div class="blog-step-body">
        <p>Once the track is clean, the mechanism is lubricated, and the door is aligned, test the lock slowly. Lift the handle fully to engage the bolts. Turn the key smoothly without forcing it. If it locks and unlocks cleanly, you’ve solved it. If the handle still sticks, spins freely, or the key won’t turn, the internal gearbox or cylinder has likely failed. At this point, stop manipulating it and book a qualified locksmith. Forcing a broken multipoint system can damage the frame and compromise your home security.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="blog-tips-block">
      <h2>Practical Advice to Prevent Future Lock Jams</h2>
      <ul>
        <li>Clean patio door tracks every season. Grit and leaves are the main cause of roller wear and misalignment.</li>
        <li>Lubricate locks and hinges twice a year with silicone spray. It keeps mechanisms smooth without attracting dirt.</li>
        <li>Lock doors gently. Slamming or yanking the handle strains the gearbox and bends locking bolts.</li>
        <li>Check hinge screws annually. Loose hinges allow the door to drop, which quickly leads to jamming.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>

    <div class="blog-when-block">
      <h2>When to Call an Emergency Locksmith</h2>
      <p>You’ve done the sensible checks, but some faults need a qualified tradesman straight away. Book an emergency callout if:</p>
      <ul>
        <li>The handle spins freely but the lock won’t engage</li>
        <li>The key snaps inside the cylinder or won’t turn at all</li>
        <li>The door has dropped severely and won’t close properly</li>
        <li>You hear grinding or clicking from inside the lock mechanism</li>
        <li>You need a same day repair to secure your home before nightfall</li>
      </ul>
      <p>Never drill out a multipoint lock or attempt to replace the gearbox unless you’re trained. Incorrect fitting leaves doors insecure and can void home insurance. Let a certified locksmith handle the repair or replacement safely.</p>
    </div>
