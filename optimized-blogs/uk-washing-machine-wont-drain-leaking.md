There is a very specific kind of domestic defeat that happens on laundry day. You press start, walk away feeling productive, and return to find your washing machine full of soapy water, humming sadly like it’s given up on life. You open the door. A tidal wave threatens the kitchen floor. You check underneath. There’s a puddle spreading towards the fridge. At this point, you’re not just dealing with a faulty appliance; you’re negotiating with a small, wet rebellion. I’ve stood in that exact kitchen, socks soaking through, wondering if I should start bailing water with a measuring jug. But before you start tipping the machine on its side or ringing an emergency tradesman in a panic, take a steady breath. Washing machines that won’t drain or leak are almost always caused by a blocked filter, a kinked waste hose, or a simple pump jam. With a calm head and a few safe checks, you can often clear the blockage and stop the leak yourself. Let’s walk through it step by step, so you can save your floor, save your laundry, and avoid an unnecessary callout.

<div class="blog-step-card">
  <div class="blog-step-header">
    <span class="blog-step-number">Step 1</span>
    <h2>Pause the Cycle & Isolate Power and Water</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="blog-step-body">
    <p>If the drum is full of water or leaking, stop the machine immediately. Turn the dial to “Off” or press the power button. Unplug it from the wall. Never reach into water or tilt the machine while it’s plugged in. Next, turn off the cold water supply tap behind or beside the machine. This stops more water from entering and prevents the leak from worsening. Lay old towels around the base to soak up spills. If water has reached nearby sockets or the consumer unit, do not touch them. Call a qualified electrician if you suspect electrical exposure.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="blog-step-card">
  <div class="blog-step-header">
    <span class="blog-step-number">Step 2</span>
    <h2>Check & Clean the Pump Filter</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="blog-step-body">
    <p>Most UK washing machines have a small access panel at the bottom front. Open it carefully (some water will spill out, so have a shallow tray or towels ready). Unscrew the filter slowly, letting water drain into your tray. Pull the filter out completely and check for coins, hair grips, lint, or small socks. These are the usual suspects for pump blockages. Rinse the filter under warm water, wipe the housing clean, and screw it back in tightly. A loose filter is a common cause of leaks, so ensure it’s seated properly before testing.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="blog-step-card">
  <div class="blog-step-header">
    <span class="blog-step-number">Step 3</span>
    <h2>Inspect the Waste Hose for Kinks or Blockages</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="blog-step-body">
    <p>Pull the machine out gently (get help if it’s heavy) and check the grey waste hose at the back. Look for sharp bends, crushing, or kinks that could stop water flowing. Straighten the hose carefully. If it connects to a sink spigot or standpipe, detach it and check for sludge, detergent buildup, or debris. Run warm water through the hose into a bucket to clear light blockages. Reattach it securely with the hose clip. Ensure the hose isn’t pushed too far down the standpipe, as this can cause siphoning and constant draining.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="blog-step-card">
  <div class="blog-step-header">
    <span class="blog-step-number">Step 4</span>
    <h2>Check the Sink U-Bend & Spigot</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="blog-step-body">
    <p>If your washing machine drains into a kitchen sink spigot, the blockage might not be in the machine at all. Run the kitchen tap. If it drains slowly or gurgles, the U-bend is likely clogged with grease and food waste. Place a bucket underneath, unscrew the U-bend, and clear out the buildup. Reassemble tightly. Once the sink drains freely, test the washing machine drain again. Many “machine faults” are actually shared plumbing blockages masquerading as appliance failures.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="blog-step-card">
  <div class="blog-step-header">
    <span class="blog-step-number">Step 5</span>
    <h2>Run a Drain Test & Monitor</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="blog-step-body">
    <p>Once the filter is clean, the hose is straight, and the sink drain is clear, plug the machine back in and turn the water on. Select a “Drain & Spin” or “Rinse” cycle. Watch the pump area and hose connections closely. If water drains smoothly and no leaks appear, you’ve solved it. If the machine hums but doesn’t drain, displays an error code, or leaks from the base, the internal pump may be failing or a seal may be perished. At this point, stop testing and book a qualified tradesman. Forcing a faulty pump can burn out the motor or flood the kitchen.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="blog-tips-block">
  <h2>Practical Advice to Prevent Future Drain & Leak Issues</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>Clean the pump filter every 1–2 months. It takes two minutes and prevents most drainage faults.</li>
    <li>Check inlet and waste hoses annually. Look for cracks, bulges, or perished rubber. Replace if worn.</li>
    <li>Use the correct amount of detergent. Excess suds can overflow the drum and trigger leak sensors.</li>
    <li>Leave the door slightly ajar after use. This prevents mould buildup and keeps the door seal flexible.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="blog-when-block">

<div class="blog-qa-section">
  <h2>Quick Q&A: Real Questions Homeowners Ask</h2>
  <div class="blog-qa-card">
    <p class="blog-qa-q">Why won’t my washing machine drain?</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Pump filter blocked by coins, lint, or debris</li>
      <li>Waste hose kinked, crushed, or clogged with sludge</li>
      <li>Failing drain pump or blocked sink U-bend</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="blog-qa-card">
    <p class="blog-qa-q">Can I fix a leaking washing machine myself?</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Yes, if the leak is from a loose filter or kinked hose</li>
      <li>Leaks from the base, door seal, or internal tub need a pro</li>
      <li>Always isolate power and water before inspecting</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="blog-qa-card">
    <p class="blog-qa-q">Why does my machine drain constantly while filling?</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Waste hose pushed too far down the standpipe (siphoning)</li>
      <li>Hose positioned too low, creating a gravity drain</li>
      <li>Pull the hose up slightly and secure it with a clip</li>
    </ul>
