Broken Window? The US Homeowner's Guide to Emergency Boarding Up (2026 Edition)

Written by 24/7 Response Team March 21, 2026 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ 5-Star Service
Securing your property immediately is a legal duty in the US to prevent further damage and theft.
SAFETY WARNING

CRITICAL: Broken glass poses a severe laceration risk. Always use leather work gloves and ANSI-rated goggles. If you suspect structural damage, call 911 immediately.

A broken window or smashed sliding door is more than a mess; it is a critical security vulnerability and a liability risk. In the United States, homeowners have a legal "duty to mitigate" damages, meaning your insurance policy likely requires you to secure the property immediately to prevent further rain or theft damage. This is the definitive 2026 US Guide to Emergency Boarding Up, from choosing the right plywood to avoiding municipal city fines.

US Window Mitigation: The "Knowledge Capsule" Summary

  • CDX Standard: Use 5/8" thickness CDX plywood for standard security; OSB is too moisture-sensitive for long-term use.
  • Plylox Clips: Use tension-based clips for brick or stone casings to avoid drilling into the home's exterior.
  • Blight Ordinances: Many US cities fine homeowners $100-$500 per day for "perpetual boarding" without a permit.
  • Insurance Clause: Most HO-3 policies fully reimburse the cost of professional boarding under the "Reasonable Repairs" section.

1. The Survival Priority: Safety First

Before picking up a hammer, you must address the immediate physical hazards. Modern US homes often use tempered glass (which crumbles) or older plate glass (which shatters into lethal shards). Wear heavy-duty leather work gloves and ANSI-rated safety goggles. Never use a standard kitchen vacuum on large shards; use a shop-vac with a HEPA filter.

A broken window is an invitation. If you cannot board it up within 2 hours, you should have someone stay at the property to maintain security and prevent "attractive nuisance" liability claims.

Professional security boarding prevents secondary damage and satisfies your "Duty to Mitigate" for insurance coverage.

2. Choosing the Right Materials: OSB vs. Plywood

In the US, the choice depends on your local climate. OSB (Oriented Strand Board) is the cheapest option ($15–$25 per 4x8 sheet) but acts like a sponge. If it rains, the edges will swell and delaminate, losing structural integrity within 48 hours.

CDX Plywood is the "Standard" for professional boarding ($35–$50). The 'X' stands for exposure, meaning it can handle some rain. Use 5/8" thickness or 3/4" for maximum security in urban areas like Chicago or Los Angeles.

Expert Tip: If you are in a hurricane zone (Florida, Texas), use 3-inch deck screws and ensure boards overlap the frame by at least 2 inches to manage the internal pressure that can lift roofs off.

3. Municipal Law: Avoiding the "Blight" Fine

In major US municipalities, long-term boarding up can trigger city fines. "Blight Ordinances" are designed to prevent neighborhood decay. If boards are up for more than 30 days without an active building permit, you may face daily fines. Some cities now require "Clear Boarding" (polycarbonate), which is significantly more expensive but satisfies police visibility requirements.

Once the window is boarded, ensure all other entry points are secured with ANSI Grade 1 deadbolts.

4. US Window Maintenance Checklist 2026

  • Check IGU (Insulating Glass Units) for seal failure in April.
  • Lubricate sliding door tracks with dry silicone spray twice a year.
  • Verify EPA Lead-Safe compliance for all work on pre-1978 homes.
  • Audit PIR security lighting in September before the winter dark.
