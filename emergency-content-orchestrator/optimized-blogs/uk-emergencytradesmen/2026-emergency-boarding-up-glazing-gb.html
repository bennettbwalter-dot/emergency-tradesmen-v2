1. The UK Context: uPVC vs. Timber Frames

The majority of UK residential properties built after 1980 utilise uPVC (Unplasticized Polyvinyl Chloride) window and door frames. These frames are hollow and reinforced with internal steel. If you screw plywood directly into the white plastic, you destroy the thermal integrity and the internal drainage channels.

Once a uPVC frame is drilled, it usually needs to be replaced entirely, turning a £150 glass repair into a £1,000 window replacement. The solution is the "Batten" or "Sandwich" method, which clamps the boards onto the frame without using screws.

Professional security boarding uses subframe attachments to prevent damage to existing window frames.

2. The "Sandwich" Boarding Method (Non-Destructive)

This is the professional standard for UK emergency glaziers in 2026. It involves cutting two sheets of 9mm or 12mm OSB/Plywood—one for the inside and one for the outside.

By placing 2x1 timber battens horizontally across the middle of both boards and passing a Coach Bolt through the centre, you compress the boards against the frame. This creates a secure, watertight seal with zero damage to the plastic or wood.

Expert Tip: For high-rise flats, boarding must happen from the inside out using specialized clips or pre-cut internal frames to avoid the cost of scaffolding or cherry pickers.

3. High-Security Options: Steel Screening (Sitex)

For empty properties or those in high-crime areas of London, Manchester, or Birmingham, plywood is often insufficient. Perforated steel screens (Sitex) provide a fire-proof barrier that cannot be cut with a standard saw. They also allow airflow, preventing the damp and mould issues common in the UK's humid climate during long-term vacancies.
