## 2. Common Points of Failure in US EVSE

The most frequent emergency calls involve the J1772 or NACS (Tesla) charging handle itself. High-frequency use can lead to broken pins or damaged locking mechanisms that prevent the vehicle from communicating with the charger.

Internally, the contactor—the high-power relay that snaps the power on—is a common wear item. If you hear a "double click" or a loud buzzing from the wall unit, the contactor is likely failing and could weld shut, creating a severe safety risk to the vehicle's battery management system.

<blockquote class="expert-tip">
<p>If you see an "Error" light on your charger, check the temperature of the wall plug. If it's too hot to touch, you have a high-resistance connection that could lead to a structural fire. Shut off the dedicated breaker in your load center immediately and call a professional electrician.</p>
</blockquote>

## 3. Dealing with Logic Board and Smart Failures

Modern "Smart" chargers rely on Wi-Fi and cloud connectivity to function. In 2026, logic board failures caused by power surges or firmware corruption are increasingly common. These faults often require a specialized technician to perform a hard reset or a component-level replacement.

Connectivity issues can sometimes be resolved with a mesh network upgrade, but if the internal radio module has failed, the unit will often default to a "Restricted" mode that only provides minimal power, lengthening charging times exponentially.
