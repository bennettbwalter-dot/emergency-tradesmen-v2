<main>
    <p>In the UK residential security audit of 2026, a disturbing trend has emerged: homeowners are installing world-class anti-snap locks into structurally inferior door frames. While the lock remains unbreachable, the frame itself is being exploited. Known as "Frame Spreading," this technique involves using a simple lever or air-wedge to pull the lock's bolts out of the plastic frame. With 45% of UK burglaries now involving direct damage to the door or frame, the "Hollow Frame Trap" is the \#1 security oversight for properties using 1990s or early 2000s uPVC technology. As we align with the PAS 24:2026 standards, understanding how to reinforce the "Soft Underbelly" of your doorway is essential. This guide outlines the structural upgrades required to make your uPVC or composite door truly secure.</p>

    <h2>1. The "Knowledge Capsule" Summary: UK Frame Defense 2026</h2>
    <div class="capsule-box">
        <ul>
            <li><strong>The Core Threat:</strong> Hollow uPVC frames lack the rigidity to resist spreading, allowing bolts to "pop" out of the strike plate.</li>
            <li><strong>The PAS 24 Milestone:</strong> Modern 2026-spec frames must include internal steel reinforcement and resistance to thermal-distortion attacks.</li>
            <li><strong>The "Dog Bolt" Fix:</strong> Hinge-side security pins prevent the door from being lifted or levered even if the hinges are cut.</li>
            <li><strong>Glazing Security:</strong> Verify your glass is internally beaded to prevent thieves from simply removing the window from the outside.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
    <p>By 2026, the era of the "Simple Plastic Door" is over. High-performance composite doors and steel-reinforced uPVC are the new UK benchmarks. However, millions of UK homes still rely on "Legacy Frames" that are effectively hollow plastic boxes. In a 2026 security scenario, your frame's ability to resist 4kN of spreading force is what keeps your family safe. We provide the technical structural audits to identify these hollow traps and provide a hardened path to compliance.</p>

    <h2>2. What is "Frame Spreading"? The Quietest Forced Entry Method</h2>
    <div class="capsule-box">
        <ul>
            <li><strong>The Leverage Point:</strong> Burglars insert a crowbar or a heavy-duty inflatable wedge between the door and the frame near a locking point.</li>
            <li><strong>The Flex:</strong> Unreinforced uPVC can flex up to 30mm—enough for the locking bolts to disengage from the metal strike plate.</li>
            <li><strong>The Entry:</strong> The door swings open without a single lock being broken, often leaving very little visual evidence of the crime.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
    <p>Frame spreading is the "Quiet Professional" of burglary techniques. Unlike lock snapping, it generates almost no noise. In 2026, we see this method used in high-density UK estates where traditional noisy break-ins would be noticed. If your door frame rattles in the wind or feels "spongy" when pulled, it is a prime candidate for a spreading attack. A 2026-standard frame is engineered to be rigid enough that the wedge fails long before the plastic bends.</p>

    <blockquote class="expert-tip">
        <p>Expert Tip: Check your strike plates (the metal parts the bolts go into). In a secure 2026 setup, these screws should go through the plastic and bite into the internal steel-reinforcement channel. If they only bite into the white uPVC, the frame will spread in seconds under load.</p>
    </blockquote>

    <h2>3. The PAS 24:2026 Shift: New Thermal and Mechanical Attack Standards</h2>
    <div class="capsule-box">
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Thermal Resistance:</strong> The latest PAS 24 updates include testing against "Freezing Sprays" that make plastic brittle and easier to shatter.</li>
            <li><strong>Twisting Loads:</strong> New tests simulate the high-torque twisting of the frame to mimic the modern professional-grade levers used by thieves.</li>
            <li><strong>Installation accountability:</strong> 2026 standards demand that the frame's performance as tested is maintained by the qualified installer on-site.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
    <p>PAS 24 is no longer just a "Minimum Requirement"—in 2026, it is the primary defense against evolving criminal tools. The shift toward PAS 24:2022+A1:2024 (enforced in 2026) recognizes that burglars are no longer just using hammers; they are using chemistry and hydraulics. When we install a 2026-spec door, we are installing a system that has been "Lab-Tortured" to resist these exact scenarios. If your door lacks a PAS 24 certificate, you are relying on luck, not engineering.</p>

    <h2>4. uPVC vs. Timber vs. Composite: Structural Integrity Compared</h2>
    <div class="capsule-box">
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Timber:</strong> Naturally rigid but susceptible to weather-rotting at the hinge points, which can weaken the "Bite" of security screws.</li>
            <li><strong>uPVC (Standard):</strong> Excellent weatherproofing but structurally weak unless reinforced with internal steel or aluminium box-sections.</li>
            <li><strong>Composite:</strong> The 2026 "Heavyweight Champion." Combines a solid timber/foam core with a GRP (Glass Reinforced Plastic) skin for maximum rigidity.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
    <p>The UK moved to uPVC in the 1980s for its "Low Maintenance" appeal, but security was often a secondary thought. By 2026, we are seeing the "Composite Revolution." A composite door in a reinforced frame is 10x more resistant to spreading than a standard 1990s uPVC unit. If you are upgrading your home in 2026, the ROI on a composite structural upgrade is nearly 100% when considering both security and thermal efficiency.</p>

    <div class="fun-fact">
        <p><strong>Fun Fact!</strong> The "U" in uPVC stands for "unplasticised." This means it hasn't been softened, making it rigid and durable. Interestingly, a high-quality uPVC frame can be recycled up to 10 times, giving a single piece of material a potential 350-year security lifespan!</p>
    </div>

    <h2>5. The "Dog Bolt" Solution: Securing the Hinge Side</h2>
    <div class="capsule-box">
        <ul>
            <li><strong>What are they?</strong> Heavy-duty steel pins that project into the frame on the hinge side when the door is closed.</li>
            <li><strong>The Function:</strong> Even if a thief uses a hacksaw to cut through your hinges, the "Dog Bolts" keep the door leaf locked firmly in the frame.</li>
            <li><strong>Installation:</strong> We recommend at least 2 pairs of dog bolts (top and bottom) for any door protecting a main entrance.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
    <p>Many homeowners focus entirely on the lock side and forget the hinges. The hinges are a major pivot point that can be smashed or unscrewed. In 2026, the "Dog Bolt" is the silent protector of the hinge side. It transforms the door from a "Flap" into a "Planted Barrier." These inexpensive additions are the single most effective "Bang-for-your-Buck" security upgrade we offer for legacy uPVC frames.</p>

    

    <h2>6. Internal Glazing vs. External Beads: Preventing Glass Removal</h2>
    <div class="capsule-box">
        <ul>
            <li><strong>The Vulnerability:</strong> Many older uPVC doors have the glass held in by "Beads" on the outside. A thief can simply pop these off and remove the entire glass pane silently.</li>
            <li><strong>The 2026 Standard:</strong> All secure doors should be "Internally Glazed." This means the beads are on the inside, making them inaccessible from the street.</li>
            <li><strong>Security Tape:</strong> Modern 2026 frames also use structural silicones or high-bond glazing tapes to fuse the glass to the frame.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
    <p>If you can see the horizontal plastic strips holding your glass in from the outside, your door is a liability. It doesn't matter how many locks you have if the burglar can remove the glass in 60 seconds. In 2026, our first step in a "Home Hardening" audit is checking the glazing beads. If yours are external, we can retro-fit security clips or recommend a "Security-Sealed" glass replacement that meets active PAS 24 standards.</p>

    <h2>7. Reinforcement Audit: Checking for Steel Inserts in 2026</h2>
    <div class="capsule-box">
        <ul>
            <li><strong>How to check:</strong> Open your door and look at the "Keep" area (where the lock sits). Use a strong magnet; if it sticks to the plastic, there is a steel reinforcement channel inside.</li>
            <li><strong>Why it matters:</strong> Steel prevents the plastic from deforming under the heat of a blowtorch or the leverage of a crowbar.</li>
            <li><strong>The 2026 Gap:</strong> "Budget" uPVC doors often skip this reinforcement to save cost, relying on the lock's rating instead of the frame's strength.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
    <p>A lock is only as strong as what it is screwed into. If your multi-point locking system is screwed into hollow plastic, a sturdy kick will rip the entire mechanism out of the frame. In 2026, we refuse to install high-security 3-star locks into unreinforced frames without first providing a structural warning. A secure home requires the "Solid-to-Solid" connection of steel-on-steel.</p>

    <h2>8. Strike Plate Hardening: The Difference Between Plastic and Metal</h2>
    <div class="capsule-box">
        <ul>
            <li><strong>The Weak Spot:</strong> Standard strike plates are often short and held in with two small screws.</li>
            <li><strong>The 2026 Upgrade:</strong> "Continuity Plates" or "Box Keeps" wrap around the frame and are secured with 5-7 long screws that span multiple chambers.</li>
            <li><strong>The "Anti-Bypass" Lip:</strong> Modern strike plates have an angled lip that prevents a credit card or "Shove Knife" from reaching the latch.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
    <p>The strike plate is the "Anchor" of your security. If it is shaky, your door is shaky. In 2026, we specialize in "Strike Plate Hardening"—replacing weak, legacy keeps with heavy-duty steel variants that are designed to absorb the kinetic energy of a forced entry attempt. This minor technical upgrade can increase a frame's resistance to "Direct Force" attacks by up to 200%.</p>
