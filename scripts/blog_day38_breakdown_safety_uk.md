# 7 Critical Safety Rules for an Emergency Roadside Breakdown

If your vehicle fails on a live UK carriageway, you are in immediate, high-stakes danger. To survive a roadside breakdown, pull as far onto the hard shoulder or verge as possible, exit via the passenger door, and stand well behind the safety barrier. Call a professional recovery service immediately.

> **WARNING:** Working on a vehicle at the roadside is lethally dangerous. Do not attempt to change a tyre or perform DIY repairs on a motorway or busy dual carriageway. Passing motorists failing to move over are a primary cause of roadside fatalities. Always wait for a certified recovery technician with appropriate lighting and protection.

---

## Why This Is Dangerous

![A broken down vehicle on a high-speed motorway being passed by a large HGV](/blog/day38/uk-motorway-breakdown-hard-shoulder.jpg)

The hard shoulder is not a safe zone; it is a high-risk area where the speed differential between you and passing HGVs is often 60mph or more. Every second you spend stationary near live traffic increases the risk of a "line of sight" collision. In 2024/25 alone, 14 people were killed in the UK after being struck by moving vehicles following a breakdown.

Secondary hazards include:
- **Poor Visibility:** At night or in heavy rain, your vehicle becomes nearly invisible until a driver is within metres.
- **Micro-Sleeps:** Tired drivers often drift toward stationary lights, a phenomenon known as "moth-to-flame" error.
- **High-Speed Debris:** Objects kicked up by passing traffic can be as lethal as a gunshot.
- **Infrastructure Failure:** Safety barriers are designed to absorb impact, but if you are standing in front of one, you have no protection.

## Warning Signs Your Vehicle Is About to Fail

![Electronic Throttle Control (ETC) and Check Engine warning lights on a car dashboard](/blog/day38/dashboard-warning-lights.webp)

Stay alert for these indicators to get off the road before you lose control:

- **Loss of Power:** Sudden lag in acceleration or steering weight.
- **Unusual Odours:** Acrid burning plastic or the sweet smell of coolant (antifreeze) — both require an immediate stop.
- **Warning Indicators:** Red oil or battery dashboard lights are not optional alarms; they are a ten-minute countdown to catastrophic failure.
- **Extreme Vibration:** Steering wheel wobble often indicates imminent tyre failure or critically loose wheel nuts.
- **Temperature Spike:** If the temperature needle hits the red, your engine will seize within minutes. Pull over immediately; do not "push through" to the next services.

Do not wait until you lose control before you act. The moment any of these signals appear, you must begin manoeuvring toward the hard shoulder or nearest exit.

## 7 Immediate Safety Rules (Survival Protocol)

Follow these steps exactly. Hesitation costs lives.

### 1. Check Your Surroundings
Use all mirrors before you begin to pull over. Signal early and move left progressively — do not brake suddenly. Other drivers need time to react to your actions.

### 2. Find the Refuge
On smart motorways, aim for an Emergency Refuge Area (ERA). These are marked with blue signs featuring an orange SOS telephone symbol. If you cannot reach an ERA, pull as close to the near-side safety barrier as possible. In an all-lane running (ALR) motorway, contact Highways England via SOS phone or 0300 123 5000 immediately and activate hazard lights.

### 3. Wheels Left
Once stopped, turn your front wheels toward the verge. If your vehicle is struck from behind, it will rotate away from live traffic — not into it.

### 4. Exit Passenger Side
Never open the driver's door into live traffic. Every person in the vehicle must exit through the left (passenger) side and move to the verge or behind the safety barrier immediately.

### 5. High-Vis Gear
A high-visibility vest can mean the difference between being seen in time and not. If you do not have one in your vehicle, you have identified a critical gap in your roadside emergency kit. While you wait, stay as far from the carriageway as possible and move away from your vehicle.

### 6. Stand Upstream
Position yourself *upstream* — in the direction that oncoming traffic is approaching from — so you can physically see hazards developing and move further away if a vehicle is not tracking correctly. Do not stand downstream (behind your car), where a secondary collision would push debris directly into you.

### 7. Call for Help Immediately
Use the roadside SOS telephones on motorways if your mobile has no signal. These phones put you directly through to National Highways and give your precise GPS location to emergency responders. On non-motorway roads, call Highways England on 0300 123 5000 or the emergency services on 999 if there is immediate danger.

## Rules & Regulations: IVR and SURVIVE Safety Standards

In the UK, the **Institute of Vehicle Recovery (IVR)** and the **SURVIVE Group** set the benchmark for roadside safety. These organisations dictate that recovery technicians must adhere to **NHSS17/17B** standards, ensuring they use correct PPE, amber warning beacons, and control zones before approaching any stricken vehicle.
