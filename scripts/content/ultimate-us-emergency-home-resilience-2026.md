    <h2>2. Plumbing Resilience: IPC 2026 Protocols</h2>
    <p>The **International Plumbing Code (IPC) 2026** has introduced strict new definitions for "Risk-Based Drainage." If your home is in a high-flood zone, you are now often required to have a "Monitored Backwater Valve." These aren't your grandfather's check valves; they include sensors that alert you before a sewage backup occurs.</p>
    <blockquote class="expert-tip">
        <p>Expert Tip: When your sewer backs up, a 24/7 plumber must verify that your vent stacks are clear according to <strong>IPC Chapter 9</strong>. If the vents are blocked, your toilets will "glug," indicating a pressure failure that lead to a burst pipe.</p>
    </blockquote>

    <h2>3. HVAC Evolution: The EPA Refrigerant Pivot</h2>
    <p>The US HFC Phase-Down is complete. In 2026, the cost of R-410A has spiked, making leak detection more critical than repair. Under <strong>EPA Section 608</strong>, technicians must now document "Leak Rate Thresholds." If your unit loses more than 10% of its charge annually, a professional repair is mandatory before recharging is legally allowed.</p>
    <div class="fun-fact">
        <p><strong>Did you know?</strong> 2026 HVAC units use "Spark-Proof" contactors to safely handle the new Low-GWP refrigerants, which are slightly more flammable but 80% better for the ozone layer.</p>
    </div>

    <h2>4. Electrical Safety: NFPA 70-2026 Updates</h2>
    <p>The 2026 National Electrical Code (NEC/NFPA 70) emphasizes "Surge Resilience." With the increase in smart home electronics, a single lightning strike nearby can ruin $10,000 worth of appliances. New 2026 standards require <strong>Type 1 or Type 2 SPD (Surge Protective Devices)</strong> at the main service panel.</p>
    <div class="split-dos-donts">
        <div class="dos-column">
            <h3>CAN</h3>
            <ul>
                <li>Install a whole-home surge protector to exceed basic NFPA 70 minimums.</li>
                <li>Test your GFCI outlets every month using the "Self-Test" feature required in 2026 models.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>
        <div class="donts-column">
            <h3>CANNOT</h3>
            <ul>
                <li>Ignore a "tripped" AFCI breaker. In 2026, these are sensitive enough to detect "Arcing" behind walls before a fire starts.</li>
                <li>Use old-stock breakers in a 2026 panel—they lack the diagnostic logic required by current safety standards.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>
    </div>
