**Water damage isn't just about wet carpet. It's about Category 1 (Clean), Category 2 (Grey), and Category 3 (Black) water. Knowing the difference determines whether you can dry it yourself or need a hazmat suit.**

If you have standing water in your basement, do not walk through it until you know where it came from. This guide explains the IICRC S500 standards for water damage restoration and the risks of toxic black mold (Stachybotrys).

---

## 1. The 3 Categories of Water Damage (IICRC S500)
The Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification (IICRC) defines 3 categories of water.

### Category 1: Clean Water
*   **Source:** Burst supply pipe (copper/PEX), overflowing bathtub (clean), rainwater entering a broken window.
*   **Risk:** Low. You can drink this water (technically).
*   **DIY Restoration:** Possible. Use shop vacs, fans, and dehumidifiers within 24 hours.
*   **Time Limit:** After 48 hours, Category 1 turns into Category 2 as bacteria grow.

### Category 2: Grey Water
*   **Source:** Washing machine discharge, dishwasher overflow, sump pump failure, urine-tainted toilet water (no feces).
*   **Risk:** Moderate. Contains chemical or biological contaminants that cause illness.
*   **DIY Restoration:** Risky. You must wear PPE (gloves/boots). Carpet padding must be replaced. Carpet can be salvaged if sanitized.
*   **Time Limit:** Turns into Category 3 rapidly.

### Category 3: Black Water
*   **Source:** Sewage backup, toilet overflow with feces, flood water from rivers/streams (contains agricultural runoff/dead animals), standing water older than 3 days.
*   **Risk:** High. Contains gross pathogens (E. coli, Hepatitis, Salmonella).
*   **DIY Restoration:** **NEVER.** Porous materials (drywall, carpet, insulation, wood) *must* be removed and discarded. You cannot "clean" Category 3 carpet. It must be cut out.

## 2. The Mold Timeline (24-48 Hours)
Mold spores are everywhere. They just need moisture to grow.
*   **0-24 Hours:** Water wicks into drywall, insulation, and framing. Materials swell.
*   **24-48 Hours:** Mold spores germinate. Musty odors begin.
*   **48-72 Hours:** Visible mold growth appears on organic materials (paper facing of drywall, wood studs).
*   **7 Days:** Structural rot begins. Biofilm forms.

### Stachybotrys Chartarum (Black Mold)
*   **Appearance:** Slimy, dark black/green patches.
*   **Toxins:** Produces mycotoxins (trichothecenes) that cause respiratory issues, bleeding in the lungs, and neurological damage.
*   **Removal:** Requires containment (plastic sheeting) and Negative Air Pressure machines to prevent spores spreading to the rest of the house.

---

## 3. The 4 Classes of Water Intrusion
Categories describe the *type* of water. Classes describe the *volume* and *evaporation load*.

*   **Class 1:** Slow evaporation. Only part of a room is wet. Little absorption into carpet/pad.
*   **Class 2:** Fast evaporation. Entire room wet. Water wicked up walls < 24 inches. Carpet and pad saturated.
*   **Class 3:** Fastest evaporation. Ceiling leak saturating insulation, walls, and floors. Everything is wet.
*   **Class 4:** Specialty drying. Water trapped in bound materials (hardwood floors, plaster, concrete). Requires specialized heat drying equipment.

---

## 4. Remediation Protocol: Step-by-Step
If you hire a pro (Servpro, Paul Davis, Local Restoration), here is what they will do.

1.  **Extraction:** Removing standing water with truck-mounted pumps.
2.  **Demolition (Strip Out):** Cutting wet drywall 2 feet above the water line. Removing wet insulation. Pulling wet carpet/pad.
3.  **Sanitization:** Applying antimicrobial agents (Microban) to studs and concrete.
4.  **Drying:** Setting up **Air Movers** (fans) to evaporate water from materials and **Dehumidifiers** (LGR) to pull moisture from the air.
5.  **Monitoring:** Daily checks with moisture meters until wood hits < 15% Moisture Content (MC).

## 5. FAQ: Water Damage & Insurance

**Q: Does bleach kill mold?**
A: **No.** Bleach removes the color from surface mold on tile, but it does not penetrate porous materials like drywall or wood. The water in the bleach actually feeds the roots of the mold deep inside. Use a specialized fungicide (Concrobium) or Hydrogen Peroxide.

**Q: Will insurance pay?**
A: **Yes, usually.** If the water damage is "Sudden and Accidental" (burst pipe).
    *   **Caveat:** They often deny "Gradual Damage" (a slow leak you ignored for months) or "Flood" (rising water from outside) unless you have separate Flood Insurance (FEMA).

**Q: Can I save my hardwood floors?**
A: **Maybe.** If it is Clean Water (Cat 1) and you act within 24 hours. A "Mat Drying System" can suck moisture through the wood. If the planks start "cupping" (edges curling up), they often lay flat after drying. If they "crown" (center bows up), they are ruined.

**Q: Is the smell dangerous?**
A: **Yes.** A musty smell means active mold growth (MVOCs - Microbial Volatile Organic Compounds). If you smell it, do not sleep in that room.

---

## Summary
Treat every water leak as a ticking clock. **Category 1 (Clean)** is a DIY fix. **Category 3 (Sewage)** is a Hazmat event. Act within **48 hours** to prevent mold. If in doubt, cut it out.
