**Panic is the enemy. In a home emergency—whether it's a burst pipe, a gas leak, or a power outage—the actions you take in the first 10 minutes determine whether the damage cost is £500 or £50,000.**

This guide is your immediate action plan. It prioritizes **Life Safety** over **Property Damage**. Do not read deep technical manuals in a crisis. Follow this protocol while you wait for the emergency tradesman to arrive.

---

## 1. The "Safety First" Triage Protocol
Before you try to fix anything, assess the danger level.

### Priority 1: Gas & Fire (Immediate Evacuation)
*   **Smell:** Rotten eggs (Mercaptan) or burning plastic.
*   **Sight:** Smoke, sparks, or flames.
*   **Action:**
    1.  **GET OUT.** Evacuate all people and pets immediately.
    2.  **Do NOT** use light switches or mobile phones inside (spark risk).
    3.  **Call 999** (UK) or **911** (US) from a safe distance.
    4.  **Do NOT** go back in for valuables.

### Priority 2: Water & Flooding (Property Damage)
*   **Sight:** Water pooling, ceiling bulging, spraying pipes.
*   **Action:**
    1.  **Stop the Source:** Turn off the main stopcock.
    2.  **Isolate Electrics:** If water is near sockets, turn off the Consumer Unit (Fuse Box).
    3.  **Call a Plumber:** Use **[Emergency Tradesmen](https://emergencytradesmen.net/)** to find a local expert.

### Priority 3: Security (Lockouts & Smashed Windows)
*   **Situation:** Key snapped, burglary, storm damage.
*   **Action:**
    1.  **Secure the Perimeter:** Is the intruder still there? Call the police.
    2.  **Temporary Fix:** Board up windows if safe to do so.
    3.  **Call a Locksmith/Glazier:** For MLA-approved secure entry.

## 2. Dealing with Water Emergencies
Water destroys a house faster than anything else except fire.

### Step 1: Shut Off the Water
*   **Internal Stopcock:** usually under the kitchen sink. Turn it **clockwise** to close.
    *   *Tip:* If it's seized, don't force it with a wrench—you might snap the spindle. Try the street valve instead.
*   **External Stop Valve:** Located in the footpath under a small lid. You may need a long "Stopcock Key."
*   **Isolation Valves:** Look for small screw-head valves on the pipes feeding the specific sink/toilet. Turn the screw so it is cross-wise to the flow.

### Step 2: Drain the System
Once the main is off, open the lowest tap in the house (usually the kitchen) and the highest tap (bathroom) to drain the pipes. This stops the leak faster.

### Step 3: Protect Electricity
If water is coming through the ceiling:
1.  **Do NOT touch the light switch.**
2.  Go to the Fuse Box.
3.  Turn off the breaker labeled "Lighting" or "Upstairs Socket."
4.  Poke a small hole in the ceiling bulge with a screwdriver to let the water drain into a bucket. This prevents the ceiling confusingly collapsing on you.

## 3. Dealing with Electrical Emergencies
Electrical fires are often silent until they are deadly.

### The Fuse Box (Consumer Unit)
Familiarize yourself with your breaker panel *now*, not in the dark.
*   **Main Switch:** The big red switch. Flipper this OFF kills power to the whole house. Do this if you are unsure where the fault is.
*   **RCD (Residual Current Device):** This safety switch trips if it detects a leak to earth (e.g., someone cutting a cable with a hedge trimmer). If it trips, assume there is a real danger.
*   **MCBs (Miniature Circuit Breakers):** These protect individual circuits (Kitchen, Lights, Shower). If one trips, leave it off.

### What to Unplug
If the power goes out:
*   **Unplug Sensitive Electronics:** Computers, TVs, and Modems. When power returns, it often comes with a "Surge" that can fry motherboards.
*   **Keep the Fridge Closed:** A fridge keeps food safe for 4 hours; a freezer for 24-48 hours. Do not open the door to "check."

## 4. Dealing with Gas & Heating
**Carbon Monoxide (CO)** is the silent killer. It is odorless and invisible.

### The Symptoms of CO Poisoning
*   Dizziness
*   Nausea ( flu-like symptoms but no fever)
*   Confusion
*   Tiredness
*   **Action:** If your alarm sounds or you feel this, Open Windows and Leave.

### Turning Off the Gas
*   **The Meter:** Located in a white box outside or under the stairs.
*   **The ECV (Emergency Control Valve):** It is a lever on the pipe.
    *   **ON:** Parallel to the pipe.
    *   **OFF:** At a right angle (90 degrees).
*   **Note:** By law, once you turn the gas supply off, it must be turned back on by a **Gas Safe Registered** engineer (UK) or licensed plumber (US) who will pressure test the system.

## 5. The "Wait Time" Checklist
You've called the emergency tradesman. They will be there in 30-60 minutes. What do you do?

1.  **Clear the Path:** Move cars from the driveway so the van can park close. Move boxes from the hallway. A tradesman carrying tools needs space.
2.  **Find the Lock Key:** If you have window locks or a locked meter cupboards, find the keys now.
3.  **Take Photos:** For your insurance claim. Photograph the damage *before* the cleanup starts.
4.  **Prepare Payment:** Emergency trades often require payment upon completion. Check if they take cards or need cash.
5.  **Comfort:** If it's freezing, put on layers. If it's flooded, put on rubber boots.

## 6. Documenting for Insurance
Your insurance company wants to pay as little as possible. Give them no excuse.

### "Trace and Access"
Standard policies cover the *damage* (the wet floor) but not the *repair* (the burst pipe). However, if you have "Trace and Access" cover, they pay for the cost of finding the leak (e.g., digging up the driveway).
*   **Keep Parts:** Don't throw away the burst pipe section. The loss adjuster acts as a forensic scientist. They need to prove it was a "sudden event" and not "gradual wear and tear."
*   **Keep Receipts:** Every penny you spend on emergency repairs, wet vac hire, or hotel rooms needs a VAT receipt.

### The "Mitigation" Clause
You have a "Duty to Mitigate." This means you must try to stop the damage getting worse.
*   **Good:** Putting a bucket under a leak. Boarding up a window.
*   **Bad:** Leaving a window open for a week while it rains because "Insurance will pay for new floors." (They won't).

## 7. FAQ: Immediate Actions

**Q: Can I use the toilet if the water is off?**
A: **Yes, once.** The cistern holds one flush worth of water. After that, you must fill the cistern manually with a bucket of water (if you have stored water) to flush again.

**Q: My pilot light has gone out. Should I relight it?**
A: **No.** If it went out, there is a reason (strong draft, thermocouple failure, or gas cut). Modern boilers don't have permanent pilots. If you have an old system, wait for a pro.

**Q: The tradesman is asking for a deposit over the phone. Is that safe?**
A: **Be cautious.** A small call-out fee is normal to reserve the slot (e.g., £50/$80). Asking for the full amount upfront is a red flag for a scam. Use verified platforms like Emergency Tradesmen to ensure legitimacy.

**Q: Who pays for the emergency call-out? Me or the Landlord?**
A: **The Landlord.** Under the Landlord and Tenant Act (UK) or Lease Laws (US), the structure, sanitation, and supply of utilities are the owner's responsibility. Pay it to safe the property, then invoice them. If they refuse, you can often deduct it from rent (seek legal advice first).

## Summary
Emergencies are stressful, but they follow a pattern. **Isolate the Utility -> Ensure Safety -> Call a Pro -> Mitigate Damage.** Memorize the location of your Stopcock and Fuse Box today. It’s the best insurance policy you have.
